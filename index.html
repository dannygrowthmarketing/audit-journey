01

The ask

Day 0: the owner asks for help.

The salon is opening new branches and wants marketing help. Before I quote anything, I audit from public data, so the first conversation is about their numbers, not my services.

  1. Sat 10 OctVisit & first conversation

    Owner explains: new salon, more branches planned, wants marketing help.

  2. Sun 11 OctPublic research

    Website source, Google listing, reviews, prices, offers, 17 competitors, search results.

  3. Mon 12 OctAudit + proposal

    27-page audit, real phone screenshots, before/after designs, live proposal site.

  4. Next30-minute walkthrough

    At the salon. The noindex fix done free, the same day.

What I ask in the first conversation

  1. What does a good month look like: revenue, bookings, new clients?
  2. Which services make the most money, and which do you want to sell more?
  3. Where do clients come from today: walk-in, Google, Instagram, referral?
  4. How do bookings happen: call, WhatsApp, walk-in, software?
  5. Do clients come back? How do you know?
  6. What have you spent on marketing, and what came back?
  7. What is fixed: hours, prices, staff, branches?
  8. What would make the next 90 days a success for you?

What I check before I reply

  • Website page source, title, robots tag, speed, every link
  • Google listing rating, reviews, hours, categories, photos
  • Prices & offers the ticket ladder and contradictions
  • Competition every salon within 1.5 km
  • Search who wins the brand, "best salon" and treatment searches
  • Social & booking Instagram, WhatsApp, booking platforms
  • Consistency phone numbers, addresses, hours everywhere

No logins needed for the first pass. Access comes only if we work together.

Marketing vs pre-marketing

What marketing can move, and what it can't.

Three layers. Pre-marketing fixes come first; marketing creates and converts demand; the salon floor turns visits into regulars. In the model, marketing alone moves roughly 45–75% of the growth.

Layer 1

Pre-marketing

Fix the foundation. Marketing on a broken base wastes money.

  • Remove noindex, real title
  • One phone number, address, hours
  • Consistent prices; no duplicate packages
  • Tracking and "how did you hear"
  • Booking flow that confirms
Layer 2

Marketing

Create and capture demand, then convert it.

  • Treatment pages, SEO, AEO
  • Google profile, review engine
  • Instagram rhythm, stylists as brand
  • Ads: hero treatments, after 7 pm, bridal
  • WhatsApp reminders and win-back
Layer 3

The salon floor

What only the business can do. Marketing can support it, not replace it.

  • Rebook at the counter
  • 2-minute consultation in the chair
  • Membership terms and pricing
  • Service quality and wait time
  • Staffing the evening slots

15

Data control

Data control: nothing enters a report without a source.

The audit uses six evidence labels. Reports after the contract use the same discipline with the client's own data.

Verified

Read directly from a public source on a date

Observation

Seen when tested; may change

Inference

A reasoned conclusion, not a fact

Unknown

Needs the business's data or access

Illustrative

Hypothetical example, never the client's numbers

Reconstruction

Redrawn from live content, not a screenshot

Rules I work by

  1. Every number has a source and a date.
  2. Platform numbers are checked against billing before they are believed.
  3. Where data doesn't exist, the report says so; nothing is filled in.
  4. Models are labelled illustrative and every input is adjustable.
  5. The client owns every account; I work as a user.

Tracking I set up in month 1

  • GA4 events: WhatsApp click, call click, booking started, booking confirmed
  • Search Console indexing, queries, pages
  • Tagged links one WhatsApp link per campaign and channel
  • Counter question "how did you hear of us?" for 30 days
  • One sheet bookings, source, service, stylist, bill, returned?

Limits of this audit: no internal data. Client numbers, revenue, spend and booking rates are not claimed. Instagram, the Meta Ad Library and Google profile photos couldn't be read automatically and are marked Unknown.

Before we startWhy should we let you audit us before we agree anything?

Because the audit uses only public data and costs you nothing to read. For Arise & Shine it found the website was telling Google not to list it. You should know that whether you hire me or not.

Before we startWhat do you need from us for the audit?

Nothing for the first pass. I read your website, Google listing, reviews, prices and the competition from public sources. Access comes later, only if we work together.

Before we startHow long does an audit take?

Two to three days for a single-location business. For Arise & Shine: a visit on Saturday, public research on Sunday, before/after screens and the proposal on Monday.

Before we startWill you tell us things we already know?

Some. The value is in the order: which leak costs the most, which fix is cheapest, and what to do first. I rank everything by impact and effort.

Before we startIs this a sales pitch disguised as an audit?

The audit stands on its own. Every finding is labelled Verified, Observation, Inference or Unknown, so you can check it yourself. The proposal is one page at the end.

Market & audienceHow do you research our market?

I map every competitor in the catchment: rating, review count, hours, price signals and what their reviews praise. For Arise & Shine that was 17 salons within 1.5 km, six of them within 400 m.

Market & audienceWho is our real competition?

The salons a client passes on the way to you, and the ones that win the same searches. Adorn & Admire, 360 m away with 3,580 reviews, is the biggest threat on volume.

Market & audienceHow do you decide which customers to go after?

From evidence, not personas. Your hours (till 10 pm), valet parking and premium treatments point to evening professionals, frizz-treatment clients, bridal season and men's grooming.

Market & audienceShould we try to attract everyone?

No. Deep discounting attracts clients who leave for the next offer. I target clients who value the room, the stylist and the late hours, and who will come back.

Market & audienceWhat makes us different from the salon next door?

Open till 10 pm daily, valet on a tight street, Davines and Olaplex treatments, and a published price list. Most rivals close at 8–9 pm and don't publish prices.

Website & web designDo we need a new website?

Not first. Your site looks good and has a real price list. It needs to be switched on for Google, fixed on the first screen, and given a page per treatment.

Website & web designWhat if we don't have a website at all?

Then the Google Business Profile is the website for the first month: services with prices, photos, booking link. A fast one-page site with WhatsApp booking follows, then treatment pages.

Website & web designWhat is wrong with our homepage?

The first screen is a generic sentence, counters that show "0 +", buttons split by gender instead of need, and a chat pop-up that covers the headline. The best offer is two pages away.

Website & web designHow do you decide what goes on the first screen?

What the client needs to decide in ten seconds: what you're best at, proof (★4.9), the reason to pick you (open till 10, valet), and one clear way to book.

Website & web designWill a redesign change how our brand looks?

No. I keep your logo, colours and tone. The changes are in order, clarity and proof, shown as before/after screens before anything is built.

SEO, AEO & contentWhy don't we show up on Google?

Your homepage carries a "noindex, nofollow" tag that tells search engines not to list it. Searching your own name returned other salons' booking pages instead.

Treatment and price searches like "hair botox price Kalyan Nagar" that no salon owns today, plus "salon near me" after 7 pm. Your price list is the best SEO asset you have.

SEO, AEO & contentWhat content should we post?

Before/afters with the stylist's name, stylist spotlights, weekend slots, and client reels with consent. People book a person, not "our professionals".
